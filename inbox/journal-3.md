# Parents
#journal #love #parents #childhood

I have been blessed from the childhood. How? I have the greatest parents I could ever ask for.

How do I define "great parents"? Well there is many factors...
But let me tell you they're not most would expect.

Growing up, we were never rich or well-off. I didn't get every toy that I wanted, I didn't go on extravagant vacations, I never had too expensive clothes, I didn't have the best computer or console, I didn't have the
latest and greatest phone, I didn't have the nicest pens and pencil case. I didn't have the trendy shoes, we didn't have a huge house with a pool and bunch of guest room that were never used, we didn't go to expensive
restaurants neither we ate exotic fruits.

We always had enough.

I was always happy, as far as I can remember, I've had a really nice childhood.

My parents were there for me, this is one of the important things.
The other important thing is that I felt loved. My mom and my dad hugged me, kissed me, told me they loved me, petted my head, napped with me, spent time with me as much as they could.
Yes there were days that life wasn't all sunshine and rainbows. Even in those days my parents taught me that I was loved no matter what, and I realize now, that was all I could've ever asked for.
I didn't need to prove that I was worth loving by showing them my worth via an external achievement. I was loved even if I failed at something.

I am really lucky. I understand this. I had both university STEM educated parents. They both come from childhoods with some level of suffering and trauma. They either couldn't spend time with their parents or were the
second choice at almost everything even though they were the one putting the most effort and trying their best. I can see those scars of their childhood in their eyes, in the words they chose, in the way they hug, in the
way they wander into thoughts. You can even see it in their advice they give it to you. In every advice they give me, I can see the child that wished he had the same advice given to them when they were younger. I am
infinitely grateful for my luck in my parents. It's some of the things you can't chose in life, and to have parents like mine is a gift.

When I talk to my friends and they talk about their parents and the problems about their relationship with their parents I can see the difference of parental figures in ones life. It affects every aspect imaginable.
Some friends don't have all parents, some have communication problems, some trust issues. Some friends even openly express their hate or dissatisfaction with their parents. I remember one friend telling me that he
never wanted to turn out like his father and another friend harshly judging her mothers personality. When I hear stuff like this, I can't relate at all, and to be clear, I do not want to relate either.

For me, both of my parents represent important aspects of life.

My father is my hero, _like every little boys,_ but usually when boys grow up they see their fathers in a different lens and stop idolizing them and instead idolize either celebrities or fictonal characters. This happens mostly because their father failed to show them how to be a man in this world. My father will always be my hero. It's not because I see him as this perfect being with nothing to fix that does no mistakes. I understand my
father is a human too. Humans are never perfect and thats what makes us unique. Humans make mistakes. My father showed me how to be a proper man. Not in a holywood like always rough macho masculine man way. He showed me
how to man up and accept your short comings and fix them, he taught me to believe in myself even when nobody believes in me, he showed me how to be comfortable with struggle, he showed me that you can change yourself, he showed me how to be a real masculine man and not those toxic and fake pseudo-masculine stuff more importantly, **he made me believe I can do everything I can put my mind to**. This is why he is my hero. He is now nearly 50 years old and he is still working as hard as a 20 year old for his goals and dreams. That's what I call _a man_. Someone who can bear any how for his why.

My mother is what I call my safe place. Without a good mother figure, people don't develop the definition of love and kindness. Shes always been the one who comforts me when times get hard, allows me to be vulnerable
when the weight of appearing bulletproof gets too heavy. My mother taught me the real definition of love. Love is to accept someone for who they are with all their strengths, shortcomings, quirks and sour sides and still
give them comfort, being there for them, believing in them, caring for them, watching out for them regardless. Real love is acceptance and care without expectations. This is what my mom taught me. She showed me I can still
be a great man while allowing myself to be vulnerable, be sad, ask for help, have emotions. My mom showed me how to really be a human. Because of her I know when I feel real love versus an imitation. This saved me lots
of times in relationships with friends and potential lovers. She also showed me how to properly love, take care of someone. When to be soft and when to be hard. How to be kind and understanding even when it's the hardest
thing to do.

Because of my parents I can go to bed every night knowing that I am enough, I can do what I put my mind to, and allow myself to be myself fearlessly.

I am forever greatful.
